# Trust Proof Reference

## 1. Familiarity

Become familiar before asking for conversion. Show up consistently in the places the audience already pays attention to: comments, useful posts, small opinions, recurring formats, and recognizable tone.

Content ideas:

- comment thoughtfully under relevant influencer/community posts
- repeat a signature viewpoint
- show daily process
- create a recognizable series

## 2. RFT: Reviews, Feedback, Testimonials

Use only real proof.

- Review: quantity and breadth of customer experience.
- Feedback: include negative feedback and how it was resolved.
- Testimonial: structured story about result, use case, and specific benefit.

Do not fake orders or fake customer reviews.

## 3. Power Customers

A recognizable customer, expert, organization, or respected user can transfer trust. If no power customer exists, use credible micro-proof first: niche expert, practitioner, serious user, or early adopter.

## 4. Partners

Trust can come from the supply chain, distributor, collaborator, school, organization, venue, or platform. Show why the partner is credible and what part of quality they guarantee.

## 5. CAD

CAD = certificate, award, diploma/document.

Use:

- licenses
- quality inspection
- food safety or product certificates
- awards
- diplomas or professional credentials
- official letters or recognitions

Explain what each document proves. Do not just post a certificate image without context.

## 6. Data

Use objective data when possible:

- number of customers served
- repeat purchase rate
- completion rate
- delivery time
- satisfaction survey
- before/after measurement with consent

State method and scope. Avoid unverifiable "100%" claims.

## 7. Influencers, KOLs, KOCs, Experts

Choose by fit, not only follower count.

- KOL: broad awareness and reputation.
- KOC: product trial and practical review.
- Expert: credibility in technical or high-risk categories.
- Celeb: mass awareness, but higher cost and brand-fit risk.

## 8. Press

Press can build third-party credibility when the outlet and story angle fit the audience. Share press links with context; do not rely on logos alone.

## 9. Negative Feedback and Crisis

Treat issues as trust opportunities only when handled honestly.

Good response:

- acknowledge issue
- state verified facts
- explain fix
- compensate fairly if appropriate
- show process improvement

Avoid creating fake scandals or manipulating outrage.
